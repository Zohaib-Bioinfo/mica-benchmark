\begin{abstract}
\noindent MICA builds protein models from cryo-EM maps by combining the density with AlphaFold~3 (AF3) predictions. I benchmarked it on \NEntries{} single-particle entries whose maps and models were released in 2026 (resolution \ResMin{}--\ResMax{}~\AA{}, \ResiduesMin{}--\ResiduesMax{} modelled residues) against ModelAngelo, EModelX(+AF) and two newer deep-learning methods, EMProt and E3-CryoFold. The entries were chosen by rules fixed before any run; every method ran with the settings of its own README on free Kaggle machines, the methods with an AF3 input got the same predictions, and one script scored every model against the deposited model. MICA built a model for \NModelMica{} of the \NEntries{} entries (two runs were killed for lack of RAM, one reached its time limit); its median TM-score was \TmMedMica{} (mean \TmMeanMica{}, 95\,\% CI \TmLoMica{}--\TmHiMica{}). On the entries where both built a model (differences are method minus MICA), EModelX(+AF) did not differ from MICA (\DiffTmEmodelx{}, 95\,\% CI \DiffLoTmEmodelx{} to \DiffHiTmEmodelx{}), EMProt was slightly higher (\DiffTmEmprot{}, \DiffLoTmEmprot{} to \DiffHiTmEmprot{}; Holm $p$ \PwHolmTmEmprot{}), E3-CryoFold was lower (\DiffTmEthree{}; Holm $p$ = \PwHolmTmEthree{}) and ModelAngelo was lower on the maps above 3.0~\AA{} (\DiffTmAngeloHigh{}, \DiffLoTmAngeloHigh{} to \DiffHiTmAngeloHigh{}; Holm $p$ = \PwHolmTmAngeloHigh{}) but not on the maps up to 3.0~\AA{}. Free compute forced two deviations from MICA's own pipeline, a coarse docking search and the omission of its real-space refinement, which the Phenix version available here would not run; both are described with their probable effects. The sample is small, and the conclusions apply to maps of up to \ResiduesMax{} modelled residues. Code, the frozen entry list, environments and the raw per-entry metrics are public.
\end{abstract}
